# Break-window statistics

The tip occupies the main area. A small break-type label, timer/controls and four statistics rows complete the window. Postpone has a +1, +2 badge counting presses in this break; the badge appears when its reminder returns, with a short CSS animation that respects reduced-motion settings. No postpone duration or scheduled-start text is shown.

| Row | Today | 7-day avg/day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Break time | Actual timer and qualifying-idle time combined, without overlap. | Combined time across today and the previous six local dates, divided by seven. |
| Without delay | Completed breaks with zero or one Postpone, divided by all resolved breaks. Additional postponements, skips and early endings count against the percentage. | The same rate across all resolved breaks in the seven-day window. |
| Average stretch | Mean ended work stretch, excluding running breaks, qualifying idle and Pause. | Mean of all ended work stretches in the seven-day window. |
| Computer use | Tracked time while the app is running, excluding breaks, qualifying idle, Pause, suspension and schedule exclusions. | Seven-day total divided by seven, including inactive days as zero. |

A dash means no applicable breaks or work stretches yet. Computer use is an estimate, not proof of productive work. Short idle below the configured threshold remains computer use; idle cannot be excluded when monitoring is disabled. Waiting and Postpone delays count as computer use. Partial timer time is retained even if a break is later postponed or ended.

Only seven local calendar dates are retained in `stretchly-statistics.json` beside the profile's `config.json`. Older dates, lifetime totals and removed metrics are discarded on load and while running. Both the main file and its recovery backup are rewritten with the pruned data; records are never inferred for time while the app is closed. Existing daily records within seven days migrate automatically. No input or application history is recorded.

Storage remains bounded and local. Changes are combined into a five-second save, with a five-minute checkpoint and normal-Quit flush. The backup contains the same current seven-day summary; abrupt termination may lose the newest unsaved changes. Break-window figures refresh at most once per second, sharing the main-process snapshot across monitors.

Break debt uses a separate balance in the same saved file. Each active minute earns the schedule's own larger-break average: one full Extended cycle contains one Extended break plus `extendedBreakInterval - 1` Long breaks, spread over `extendedBreakInterval × microbreakInterval × (breakInterval + 1)` active minutes, and that rest total divided by that cycle length is the owed rate. The short countdown while a larger-break window is waiting to start is excluded; active time during a postponement still earns rest. With the default 10-minute Mini interval and a 3-minute Long / 15-minute Extended cycle, each active minute earns 14 seconds of larger-break rest. Qualifying AFK and the elapsed portion of a Long or Extended timer pay down that balance. Overlap is credited once. Mini timers are excluded from the debt balance unless their time is also part of qualifying AFK. **Skipping any break is not a debt event**. This covers tray **Skip current break**, all three **Skip to the next ...** entries, **Reset breaks** and an early Finish of a break window: the abandoned window earns no rest credit, the postponement balance survives, and the outstanding amount is re-forecast onto the next larger breaks. Skipping never clears the three-break repayment plan either. The skip still counts as a completed-but-skipped break for Without delay % where the tray Skip is used.

The app projects rest through the end of the current 120-minute work block when the schedule changes. Those 120 active minutes are only the projection horizon, not the rate denominator. It adds make-up time to the next three larger breaks **in proportion to their base lengths** (a 4/4/12 sequence splits 1:1:3), and the returning break never receives more than its own slot. A postponement updates the forecast when the break returns. The balance, the outstanding postponement debt and the three-break repayment plan are all written to `stretchly-statistics.json`, so **Total time debt survives midnight, Quit and restart**; only the break window's own Debt in this break figure is session-only, because the window itself is not restored. Existing profiles without a `postponement` field start with none. Time while the app is closed is excluded. The balance carries across days if you stop before taking the remaining rest.

Audit note (2026-11-14): the ledger is cumulative, not a sliding two-hour history, and the 120-active-minute `workBlock` is only the forecast horizon. The owed rate comes from the real schedule cycle, so an untouched day, a day with skips and a day with postpones all target the same average larger-break rest: `R + B = r·W` holds exactly and `r` equals the schedule's own larger-break average. `scripts/check-break-average.mjs` enforces this over 400 randomized setting sets plus 200 live-profile days and fails if the rate drifts, if rest is lost or invented, or if the ideal daily average is not reached. Debt carried to tomorrow counts as today's rest; Mini breaks are real rest but are not debt events, so they are excluded from `r` and included in the daily total.

Active work during a larger-break postponement is guaranteed to be repaid even when previous rest left a credit: the earned amount is a floor for the **total** owed, then spread over the next three larger breaks by their base lengths instead of being charged entirely to the returning break. While that postponement is outstanding, Total time debt shows at least that earned amount. The amount is already part of the balance, so it is never added twice. **No skip-to, reset or early finish can clear it**: `replan()` discards only the forecast, not the unpaid rest, and `resolve()` consumes a repayment slot only when that break actually served its assigned share. An unfinished break keeps the whole plan and the postponement, to be re-forecast at the next schedule change. When a break does serve its slot, only that share of the postponement is retired: the two remaining slots stay owed and are repaid by the next two larger breaks, so a credit from earlier rest cannot make them disappear. At that point the postponement's accrual is frozen and the frozen state is persisted, so ordinary work afterwards is counted once in the balance rather than being charged again on top of the plan.

An early qualifying AFK restarts the current interval and preserves the upcoming larger break. AFK that overlaps its due time or ends within one minute of it can fulfill that break if it meets the base duration. An absence that begins during a running Long or Extended break and lasts at least as long as that break also fulfills it, including when the break ends while away; the absence is measured from its original wall-clock start, so an OS idle-counter reset cannot shorten it. An Extended-length absence starts a fresh reminder session. The next interval starts in full at the moment of return, and one absence can fulfill at most one scheduled break. Neither type of AFK reset clears unpaid rest: replanning discards the forecast plan but keeps the postponement balance, which is re-forecast at the next schedule change.
